## Context

Sonic Adventure is definitely up there in terms of my favourite Sonic Games.
One of the aspects that elevate it is its level design, with many paths and
secrete to be found, as well as powerful movement tools. However the powerful
movement, and sometimes compact level design, has an adverse effect on game design.
Unfortunately, a lot of sections in the levels can be skipped. And as a result,
it can often turn interesting levels into effectively nothing levels.

![Sonic Adventure 1 Skips](/assets/07-rankrank/SA1Skips.gif){: .w-50 .left}

This is a large part on why I much prefer Sonic Adventure 2. While SA2 itself
also has some major skips, even if we ignore the fact that they are much harder,
there is a very simple reason I do not mind SA2 skips nearly as much. They result
in getting a bad rank.

![Sonic Adventure 2 AGDQ](/assets/07-rankrank/SA2_speedrunDrank.png){: .w-50 .left}

Games, like toys, often give you the freedom to play in many different ways.
As a result, people will play with toys the way they find the most fun.
However unlike toys, games often focus on a particular objective. And while there
are often multiple ways to achieve an objective, some of which are fun, while others
less so, players are often then less likely to play the game in the way that's most fun, and more likely to play to achieve an objective.

How is this relevant? An important aspect of game design is not just making a
game that can potentially be fun, but it is also important to design a game
to incentivize the play *to* play the game in the most fun way. While admittedly
shallow, a ranking or grading system, like that in SA2 can be used to reward/encourage
players to engage with the level design. Where as nothing in Sonic Adventure
incentivizes engaging with the level.

And this is what I want to explore: Going through most of the Sonic Games that have
a ranking system and explore how well does that game's ranking system encourages fun play,
and giving each of those games a rank.
